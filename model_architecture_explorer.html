Two small decoders — and where the recipe goes next.

Your nano_SLMs · target — 226.5M params, trained on Python code — and HuggingFace's SmolLM2-135M — 134.5M params, trained on general text — are the same architecture family: a Llama-style decoder-only transformer with GQA attention, RoPE, RMSNorm and SwiGLU. Alongside them sits Qwen3.8-Flash-Next — the open ~125B-preview of the Qwen4 architecture: the same skeleton evolved with hybrid linear attention and a 512-expert sparse MoE. Where the learning and knowledge live

"Knowledge" is not a database — it is baked into the weights during training. Interpretability research (Geva et al. 2021: FFNs behave as key–value memories; Meng et al. 2022 "ROME": factual edits succeed in mid-layer FFNs) keeps finding the same depth profile in models like these. Treat it as a rule-of-thumb, not a per-layer measurement.

Anything shared? — the two small ones: almost everything

The punchline: SmolLM2-135M and your target model are the same design (Llama-family GQA decoder). They differ in scale, vocabulary, data and training — not in how they work. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next is the next branch of the same family tree: identical skeleton (decoder-only, pre-RMSNorm, residual stream, SiLU-gated expert FFNs, causal), with evolved organs — a 3:1 linear/full attention hybrid and 512-expert sparse MoE.
